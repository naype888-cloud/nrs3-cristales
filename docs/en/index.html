Axis-step words and commutation in the cube

Summary

The nearest-neighbour lists fccBonds and bccBonds are written by hand, in the basis of primitive vectors. Can they be derived from the geometry of the cube, one step at a time? Two pull requests answer. #1987: in the coordinates of the box, the bonds of fcc and bcc, with both signs, are exactly the sums of two (fcc) or three (bcc) steps along different axes, and each neighbour is reached by exactly 2 (fcc) or 6 (bcc) ordered words. #1986: two distinct observables of the cube commute if and only if they lie on different axes. The Hamiltonian and the position of one axis do not commute, and the hypotheses of that statement cannot be dropped.

1. The idea

Two observables A, B can be of two kinds, and each has its first step.

  • They commute (AB = BA): the order of the steps does not matter. A diagonal is the sum of two steps along different axes. There are two words, x then y and y then x, and one displacement.
  • They do not commute (AB ≠ BA): this is the pair (H, X) of a single axis. Its commutator has a nonzero expectation, and by Robertson's relation the product of the variances is positive. This is where the uncertainty and the spectrum live.

3. A worked example

The bonds are written in the basis of primitive vectors. In the box, in units of half a side (fccCoord, bccCoord), each one is a word of axis steps.

latticebond in the primitive basisbox coordinatesword of steps
fcc![1, 0, 0](0, 1, 1)e₂ + e₃
fcc![0, 1, 0](1, 0, 1)e₁ + e₃
fcc![0, 0, 1](1, 1, 0)e₁ + e₂
fcc![1, −1, 0](−1, 1, 0)−e₁ + e₂
fcc![0, 1, −1](0, −1, 1)−e₂ + e₃
fcc![−1, 0, 1](1, 0, −1)e₁ − e₃
bcc![1, 0, 0](−1, 1, 1)−e₁ + e₂ + e₃
bcc![0, 1, 0](1, −1, 1)e₁ − e₂ + e₃
bcc![0, 0, 1](1, 1, −1)e₁ + e₂ − e₃
bcc![1, 1, 1](1, 1, 1)e₁ + e₂ + e₃

With the opposite signs these are the 12 and the 8 neighbours. As ordered words there are 24 and 48 (12 · 2, 8 · 3!). The box has sides a, b, c; the half side is a/2, b/2, c/2 on each axis.

4. What is proved and what is not

Proved in Lean · standard axioms only, no sorry

Everything in the table of §2. The two orders of a word and the counts 2 and 6 are decided over finite sets. The commutation statements use the Robertson relation already in Physlib.

Not claimed

These statements do not say that a Hamiltonian cannot have a diagonal hopping with an operator of its own. They say that the nearest-neighbour bonds of fcc and bcc are words of axis steps, and that steps on different axes commute. The 45° of a diagonal is a length statement (1 < √2 < √3, corpus D4), not an angle between fluctuations. The body diagonal as a single operator TxTyTz is not in Lean; only the displacement and the count of 6 words are. The nonzero-commutator statements are for the six observables (H, X) of the cube, not for an arbitrary pair of the lattice.

git clone https://github.com/naype888-cloud/nrs3-cristales
cd nrs3-cristales
lake build NRS3Cristales      # Palabras.lean, PalabrasCubo.lean

NRS3Cristales/Palabras.lean proves, for two operators on a complex inner-product space, commute_iff_forall_commutator (they commute iff ⟨[A, B]⟩ = 0 in every state) and not_commute_iff_exists_unit (they do not commute iff a unit state has ⟨[A, B]⟩ ≠ 0), with the elementary bound commutator_sq_le. It is not part of the pull requests: the cube needs only what #1986 contains. NRS3Cristales/PalabrasCubo.lean is the same cube statement written against this repository's Physlib pin.
